DEFINITIONS
D1 [method] CES: Complex exponential smoothing: a forecasting method that combines each past observation $y_t$ and its forecast error $e_t$ into the complex variable $y_t + i e_t$ and forecasts with an exponentially weighted sum of these values, $\hat{y}_t + i\hat{e}_t = (\alpha_0 + i\alpha_1) \sum_{j=1}^{t-1} (1 + i - (\alpha_0 + i\alpha_1))^{j-1} (y_{t-j} + i e_{t-j})$, or recursively $\hat{y}_t + i\hat{e}_t = (\alpha_0 + i\alpha_1)(y_{t-1} + i e_{t-1}) + (1 - \alpha_0 + i - i\alpha_1)(\hat{y}_{t-1} + i\hat{e}_{t-1})$, where $\alpha_0 + i\alpha_1$ is the [[D2:complex smoothing parameter]] and $\hat{e}_t$ carries the information on past forecast errors. [assumes C60, C62, C63, C64]
D2 [variable] complex smoothing parameter: The smoothing parameter of [[D1:CES]], the complex number $\alpha_0 + i\alpha_1$; its real part $\alpha_0$ and imaginary part $\alpha_1$ are the two real parameters estimated, and they set how much of the past observations and of the past forecast errors is carried into the forecast. [part of D1]
D3 [concept] nonlinear trend component: The state $c_t$ of the [[D1:CES]] state-space model, which carries information on both its own previous value $c_{t-1}$ and the previous level $l_{t-1}$; it does not correspond to the trend component of exponential smoothing. [part of D1]
D4 [method] seasonal CES: [[D1:CES]] extended with a second pair of level and [[D3:nonlinear trend]] states taken at lag $m$, the seasonal period, with its own [[D2:complex smoothing parameter]] $\beta_0 + i\beta_1$: $y_t = l_{0,t-1} + l_{1,t-m} + \varepsilon_t$, $l_{0,t} = l_{0,t-1} - (1 - \alpha_1) c_{0,t-1} + (\alpha_0 - \alpha_1) \varepsilon_t$, $c_{0,t} = l_{0,t-1} + (1 - \alpha_0) c_{0,t-1} + (\alpha_0 + \alpha_1) \varepsilon_t$, $l_{1,t} = l_{1,t-m} - (1 - \beta_1) c_{1,t-m} + (\beta_0 - \beta_1) \varepsilon_t$, $c_{1,t} = l_{1,t-m} + (1 - \beta_0) c_{1,t-m} + (\beta_0 + \beta_1) \varepsilon_t$. [a kind of D1]
D5 [method] SCUM: Simple combination of univariate models: the median of the forecasts of [[D9:ETS]], [[D10:ARIMA]], the [[D11:Theta method]] and [[D17:CES]]. [source B13]
D6 [method] SCUM(noCES): The median of the forecasts of [[D9:ETS]], [[D10:ARIMA]] and the [[D11:Theta method]]: [[D5:SCUM]] without CES. [a kind of D5]
D7 [method] the six compared methods: The [[D12:four compared methods]] and the two combinations, [[D5:SCUM]] and [[D6:SCUM(noCES)]]. [members D17, D9, D10, D11, D5, D6]
D8 [data] M1, M3 and tourism data: The 5,315 yearly, quarterly, monthly and other time series of the M1, M3 and tourism forecasting competitions.
D9 [method] ETS: Exponential smoothing in a state space framework with automatic selection among its models; here es() from the smooth package with model ZXZ, selecting by AICc. [source B2]
D10 [method] ARIMA: Automatic state space ARIMA; here auto.ssarima() from the smooth package. [source B15]
D11 [method] Theta method: The Theta method of forecasting; here thetaf() from the forecast package 8.14. [source B16]
D12 [method] the four compared methods: [[D17:CES with automatic seasonal selection]], [[D9:ETS]], [[D10:ARIMA]] and the [[D11:Theta method]], as the benchmark runs them. [members D17, D9, D10, D11]
D13 [method] the three benchmark methods: [[D9:ETS]], [[D10:ARIMA]] and the [[D11:Theta method]], as the benchmark runs them. [members D9, D10, D11]
D14 [variable] RMSSE: Root mean squared scaled error: the root mean squared forecast error over the horizon, scaled by the mean absolute first difference of the in-sample data; reported as its mean and median over the series. [source B17]
D15 [variable] MASE: Mean absolute scaled error: the mean absolute forecast error over the horizon, scaled by the mean absolute first difference of the in-sample data; reported as its mean and median over the series. [source B18]
D16 [variable] MSIS: Mean scaled interval score: the interval score of a prediction interval averaged over the horizon and scaled by the mean absolute first difference of the in-sample data; here of the 95% prediction interval, reported as its mean and median over the series. [source B19]

CLAIMS
C1 [formal; principal; extension; support: formal_proof E3; uses C17, C18, C19, C20, C23, C24, C25, C32, C21, C22, C33; verification unchecked]
  Depending on the value of its [[D2:complex smoothing parameter]], [[D1:CES]] is a stationary or a non-stationary model.
  extends B1, own earlier work: Exponential smoothing in a state space framework (ETS) gives 30 models with no, additive or multiplicative error, trend and seasonal components, among which a model is selected by an information criterion.
  addresses B3, own earlier work: Identifying the presence and type of trend in exponential smoothing often leads to misidentification and poorly performing long-term forecasts.
  addresses B2: The diverse trend alternatives of exponential smoothing in a state space framework make the selection of a model difficult.
  addresses B4: Selecting the most appropriate exponential smoothing model is a challenging task.
  addresses B5, own earlier work: Even when the components of exponential smoothing are identified correctly, the estimated smoothing parameters and the weights they imply can give poor forecasts.
C2 [formal; principal; support: formal_proof E3; uses C17, C18, C25; verification unchecked]
  [[D1:CES]] passes continuously from a stationary to a non-stationary model as its [[D2:complex smoothing parameter]] changes.

C5 [formal; principal; support: formal_proof E5; assumes C61; uses C16; verification unchecked]

C16 [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E1; assumes C60, C61; verification unchecked]
  [[D1:CES]] has the single-source-of-error state-space form $y_t = l_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t$, $l_t = l_{t-1} - (1 - \alpha_1) c_{t-1} + (\alpha_0 - \alpha_1) \varepsilon_t$, $c_t = l_{t-1} + (1 - \alpha_0) c_{t-1} + (\alpha_0 + \alpha_1) \varepsilon_t$, with level $l_t$ and [[D3:nonlinear trend component]] $c_t$.
C17 [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E3, cited B1, at p. 38, own earlier work; verification unchecked]
  [[D1:CES]] is stationary, with both eigenvalues of its transition matrix inside the unit circle, when $\alpha_1 < 5 - 2\alpha_0$, $\alpha_1 < 1$ and $\alpha_1 > 1 - \alpha_0$.
C18 [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E3; verification unchecked]
  When $\alpha_1 > 1$, both eigenvalues of the [[D1:CES]] transition matrix are real and one of them is greater than one.
C19 [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E3; uses C18; verification unchecked]
  When $\alpha_1 > 1$, [[D1:CES]] produces a non-stationary trajectory.
C20 [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E3; verification unchecked]
  When $\alpha_1 = 1$, [[D1:CES]] is equivalent to [[D19:ETS(A,N,N)]].
C21 [methodological; supporting; support: cited B14, own earlier work, cited B1, own earlier work]
  [[D1:CES]] is required to be stable, or at least forecastable.
C22 [methodological; supporting; support: cited B14, own earlier work, cited B1, own earlier work]

C23 [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E4, cited B1, at pp. 172-173, own earlier work; verification unchecked]
  [[D1:CES]] is stable, with all eigenvalues of its discount matrix inside the unit circle, when $(\alpha_0 - 2.5)^2 + \alpha_1^2 > 1.25$, $(\alpha_0 - 0.5)^2 + (\alpha_1 - 1)^2 > 0.25$ and $(\alpha_0 - 1.5)^2 + (\alpha_1 - 0.5)^2 < 1.5$.
C24 [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E4; uses C17, C23; verification unchecked]

C25 [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E3; uses C26, C27, C28, C29, C30; verification unchecked]
  The [[D1:CES]] point forecast $w'F^{h-1}v_t$ follows one of five trajectories set by the [[D2:complex smoothing parameter]]: a flat line, exponential growth, exponential decline, harmonic oscillation converging to zero, or diverging harmonic oscillation.

C26 [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E3; verification unchecked]
  When $\alpha_1 = 1$, the [[D1:CES]] point forecast is a flat line.
C27 [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E3; verification unchecked]
  When $\alpha_1 > 1$, the [[D1:CES]] point forecast grows exponentially.
C28 [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E3; verification unchecked]

C29 [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E3; verification unchecked]
  When $1 - \alpha_0 < \alpha_1 < (4 - \alpha_0^2)/4$, the [[D1:CES]] point forecast is a harmonic oscillation converging to zero.
C30 [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E3; verification unchecked]
  When $0 < \alpha_1 < 1 - \alpha_0$, the [[D1:CES]] point forecast is a diverging harmonic oscillation.

WARRANTS
W1 [deductive; premises C8, C31, C13, C14, C15] The seasonal model takes the level and nonlinear trend states at lag m instead of carrying a set of seasonal components, so there are no seasonal indices to renormalise. Since non-seasonal CES captures both level and trend series without a choice of trend form, the only choice left is whether to include the lag-m states.
W2 [deductive; premises C20, C26, C27, C28] With the imaginary part of the smoothing parameter equal to one CES is the local level model ETS(A,N,N); with it above one the forecasts grow exponentially, as in multiplicative trend models; and the flexibility of its trajectories lets it follow trends that the level and trend classification of exponential smoothing captures only with separate models.
W3 [deductive; premises C25] Seasonal CES consists of two CES parts, one at lag 1 and one at lag m, and each part follows one of the CES forecast trajectories, so the seasonal part can grow, decline or oscillate in its own way. The non-seasonal level can become negative while the lagged level stays positive, and the two levels evolve independently, so the seasonal part can change its amplitude and shape without any change in the level of the series.
W4 [analogical; premises C47, C5] Non-seasonal CES has an underlying ARMA(2,2) model that is stationary or not depending on its complex smoothing parameter; by analogy, the authors state the same of the SARIMA model underlying seasonal CES. No stationarity condition is derived for seasonal CES.
W5 [deductive] The count follows from the model equations: each complex smoothing parameter has two real parts, each pair of level and nonlinear trend states needs initial values (m of each for the lag-m pair), and the error variance is estimated.
W6 [inductive] On the seasonal subset of the M1, M3 and tourism data, CES with its seasonal or non-seasonal form chosen by AICc has lower RMSSE and MASE than every seasonal ETS model, which the authors take to show that the criterion chooses the seasonal form when it is needed. This is an argument from the accuracy results only: no experiment compares the selection by information criteria with other ways of choosing between seasonal and non-seasonal CES.
W7 [inductive] CES has lower RMSSE than ETS, ARIMA and the Theta method on the M1, M3, tourism and M4 data and lowers the errors of the combination it is included in, which the authors take as evidence that it is robust, while acknowledging that it does not suit every forecasting scenario. Robustness itself is not defined or tested: no experiment examines how the accuracy of CES varies across conditions or departures from its assumptions.

LIMITATIONS
L1 [model_specification; affects C3, C36, C37, C38, C39, C40; could_bias_magnitude; mitigation none] CES produces very large errors on a few series: on M1 series 47 it fails to identify the trend and its MSIS is 523.034 against 25.590 for ETS(M,N,N), which inflates its mean MSIS, and on the non-seasonal subset it fails on two series.
L2 [model_specification; affects C8, C10, C47; reduces_precision; mitigation none] The parameter restrictions under which seasonal CES is stable are not derived.
L3 [sample_size; affects C3, C4, C11, C12; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] The effect of the length of a time series on the accuracy of CES is not analysed.
L4 [model_specification; affects C16; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] CES is formulated only with an additive error term; a version with a multiplicative error term is not developed.
L5 [sampling; affects C4, C12; could_bias_magnitude; mitigation none] The 10 M4 series on which ETS broke down were excluded from the M4 comparison, so the series were selected on the failure of one of the compared methods.

OPEN QUESTIONS
O1 [from C11] Whether CES improves the SCUM combination because it captures long-term relations better than ETS, ARIMA and the Theta method, and nonlinear trends without separating level and trend, is not established.
